# AVENOR — World Location & Point-of-Interest Catalogue

> **Purpose:** A large, map-by-map catalogue of distinct places players can discover, explore, revisit, use as meeting points, scavenge, investigate, occupy temporarily or contest. This is a content blueprint, not a claim that these locations are implemented.
>
> **Scope target:** 200 named major locations across the ten proposed regions, plus smaller unnamed micro-locations, interiors and environmental discoveries. Each world receives its own identity. Reuse modular building kits and gameplay systems, not identical points of interest.

## 1. Location philosophy

AVENOR's worlds should feel dense with destinations and incidental discoveries, not like empty terrain with a few landmarks. A location earns its place through at least one meaningful purpose:
- **Explore:** distinctive architecture, layout, verticality, routes or hidden spaces.
- **Scavenge:** plausible supplies, equipment or permanent weapon discoveries, with balanced rarity.
- **Encounter:** player activity or a player-like NPC faction.
- **Establish a presence:** a suitable place to stop, gather, shelter or set up a temporary camp.
- **Contest:** a location that groups may occupy, defend, visit or challenge through ordinary gameplay.
- **Discover:** clues, records, environmental stories, unusual sights or secrets.

Not every location needs combat, loot or a reward. Quiet places, scenic overlooks and social destinations are important too. Do not turn every point into an objective marker.

## 2. What "taking over" means

AVENOR does **not** use magical permanent ownership of ordinary world locations. A group can establish a presence, stay there, use the facilities, leave supplies, set up a temporary camp where appropriate and be recognised by nearby other players or NPC faction members as the group currently present.

- Control is contextual and can change through play; it is not a permanent account entitlement.
- Existing places remain part of the world whether or not a player is present.
- A campsite or outpost does not become an invulnerable personal base.
- Player-established camps use carried/earned camp equipment and suitable terrain; no universal build-anywhere system is assumed.
- Player-like NPC factions may occupy, abandon, revisit or contest selected locations. They are sparse groups, not a background population.
- Avoid offline-raid pressure, mandatory upkeep, forced territory taxes or losing permanent weapon unlocks.
- Exact persistence, storage, PvP permissions and camp placement restrictions remain **TBD** and require separate prototypes.

## 3. Location scale and density

Use three layers:

1. **Major POIs:** named destinations with a distinct silhouette, layout and reason to visit. This catalogue targets 20 per world.
2. **Secondary POIs:** smaller named or recognisable places within a district, such as a workshop, footbridge, ranger cabin, loading yard or lookout.
3. **Micro-discoveries:** hidden rooms, roof access, crawlspaces, caches, notes, unusual objects, shortcuts, scenic details and small environmental stories.

The 200 major POIs are a long-term content target, not a launch requirement. A major POI may contain multiple interiors and micro-discoveries. Do not build 200 large bespoke structures before testing performance. Greybox a small cluster, validate the exploration loop, then expand with modular kits and authored variations.

## 4. Catalogue legend

- **Explore:** traversal, architecture, secrets or scenery.
- **Scavenge:** supplies or equipment opportunities.
- **Encounter:** player activity or player-like faction activity.
- **Presence:** suitable for a temporary player gathering/camp, subject to placement rules.
- **Contest:** player or player-like NPC faction occupation or challenge.
- **Discovery:** clue, environmental story or rare find.

Tags describe intended opportunities, not guaranteed loot or permanent rewards. Weapon discoveries remain subject to the separate permanent-unlock rules.

# 15. Cross-world location families

These are **design families**, not extra copies of the 200 named locations. They provide reusable systems and occasional smaller POIs while retaining regional identity.

- **Roadside stops:** petrol stations, diners, repair shops, rest areas, weigh stations.
- **Public services:** fire stations, clinics, ranger offices, depots, emergency shelters.
- **Transport:** bus stops, rail halts, marinas, ferry slips, cable stations, service tunnels.
- **Utilities:** substations, pumping stations, reservoirs, relay towers, waterworks.
- **Rural life:** barns, sheds, orchards, greenhouses, worker cabins, farm shops.
- **Urban life:** laundries, corner shops, courtyards, parking structures, rooftops, basements.
- **Outdoor recreation:** trail shelters, picnic areas, fishing platforms, campsites, climbing huts.
- **Restricted infrastructure:** fenced compounds, inspection buildings, research annexes, maintenance yards.
- **Abandoned places:** closed businesses, disused stations, forgotten homes, derelict workshops.
- **Small social spaces:** plazas, community halls, cafés, markets, sports grounds and public gardens.

Each instance should be adapted to the world's climate, architecture, economy and history. Avoid placing the same generic building unchanged in every region.

# 17. Occupation, camps and presence

Location tags such as **Presence** and **Contest** identify possible uses, not automatic ownership rules.

### Existing locations
- Some are public and naturally shared.
- Some are occupied by player-like NPC faction squads.
- Some are restricted, abandoned or temporarily empty.
- Some may become gathering places for real players.
- Some may be contested by real players or player-like NPC factions.

### Player-established camps
- Players may bring suitable camp equipment to approved terrain or designated camp areas.
- Camp items should have clear placement limits and server performance budgets.
- A camp can be a temporary meeting point, shelter or supply stop, not a mandatory permanent base.
- Players may invite others, leave permitted supplies and return while the relevant world state persists.
- The camp should not imply legal or permanent ownership of the surrounding land.
- Exact persistence, storage, dismantling, group access and contest rules remain TBD.

### NPC presence
- NPC groups may use selected sites as temporary camps, patrol stops, work locations or shelters.
- Their presence should follow simple schedules and world conditions.
- Groups should not instantly respawn just because a player leaves.
- Use bounded simulation so distant groups do not consume unnecessary server resources.

# 18. Location state and variation

A location can feel different across visits without needing a separate map copy. Possible states:
- Quiet / active.
- Public / restricted.
- Occupied / abandoned.
- Intact / damaged.
- Powered / without power.
- Open / closed.
- Clear weather / storm response.
- Normal season / transformed seasonal state.
- NPC faction present / faction travelling / no current faction.
- Recently visited / signs of activity / no obvious recent activity.

State changes must be authored per location and should be readable. Do not randomise important access or loot in ways that make the location feel broken.

# 19. Discovery and map knowledge

- The map may reveal major roads, settlements and known landmarks without exposing every secret.
- Players can discover POIs by travelling, observing silhouettes, following trails, hearing rumours, reading signs or finding clues.
- A discovery journal can record known places and optional details.
- Hidden locations should not all become automatic map pins.
- Players should be able to share directions socially.
- Repeat visits should remain useful through changing faction presence, world events, player encounters or alternate routes, not endlessly respawning valuable loot.

# 20. Production and performance rules

Two hundred named locations are a long-term world-content target. They must not be treated as a requirement to build two hundred bespoke buildings before testing.

Recommended production order:
1. Greybox one compact GREYHAVEN district with 8–12 varied POIs.
2. Test whether each location is distinguishable and worth visiting.
3. Build a reusable kit for one residential, one industrial, one public and one natural location family.
4. Add interiors and micro-discoveries only after traversal and performance are measured.
5. Expand GREYHAVEN to a larger cluster, then prove a visually different region.
6. Track mobile frame rate, memory, streaming, server simulation and NPC budgets throughout.
7. Use occlusion, streaming, distance-based NPC simulation and interior loading where appropriate.
8. Keep visual density high without requiring every building to be enterable.

### Per-POI production record

Every production-ready POI should eventually have:
- Unique ID and world ID.
- Name and short identity.
- Map district and approximate placement.
- Exterior footprint and vertical profile.
- Interior count and access rules.
- Traversal routes and alternate entrances.
- NPC faction use and squad schedule.
- Exploration/discovery purpose.
- Scavenge opportunities, if any.
- Presence/camp suitability, if any.
- Contest/PvP suitability, if any.
- Seasonal/global-event variants.
- Audio, lighting and environmental storytelling.
- Streaming and performance budget.
- Greybox status, art status and QA status.

# 21. Important unresolved decisions

Do not silently lock these:
- Exact world dimensions and final map count at launch.
- Which POIs are enterable versus exterior-only.
- Which locations permit PvP or temporary occupation.
- Whether camps persist through disconnects or world rotation.
- Whether player-placed objects can be stored or recovered.
- How NPC faction squads change occupation of a location.
- Whether contested sites provide services, information or access.
- How many of the 200 major POIs ship in the first world.
- Which discoveries can permanently unlock weapons.
- Whether any locations are instanced or entirely in the shared world.

**Success test:** A player should be able to travel across a region, find several genuinely different places, encounter something unexpected, choose to stay or move on, and have a good reason to explore another direction. The location catalogue exists to support that experience, not to replace it with a checklist.
