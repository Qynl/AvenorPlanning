# VIREL — Ability Sets

## Core direction

**Plant abilities are a central pillar of VIREL.** Discovering a plant should feel like discovering a new power system.

A plant does not grant one isolated perk. It unlocks an **ability set**: a themed collection of multiple abilities that express the plant's biology and give the player new ways to explore, attack and interact with the environment.

The three main gameplay pillars are:
1. **Exploration** — traversal, movement, navigation, sensing and reaching hidden places.
2. **Attacking** — spectacular plant-powered attacks used in encounters. Exact enemy types, PvP and combat rules remain open.
3. **Botanical powers** — growing, shaping, controlling and interacting with plant life and natural forces.

Each set should have a coherent identity. Some focus on movement, some on attacks, and others combine all three.

## Rarity and set progression

| Rarity | Typical set size | Intended feel |
|---|---:|---|
| Natural | 2 | Useful starter powers with clear purpose |
| Unusual | 2–3 | More specialised abilities and stronger visual identity |
| Distinct | 3 | Noticeably powerful, versatile sets |
| Ancient | 3 | Powerful abilities with interesting combinations |
| Forgotten | 3–4 | High-impact abilities and unusual mechanics |
| Relic | 4 | Very powerful, spectacular sets |
| Origin | 4+ | Signature abilities that can change how a player approaches an entire world |

These are guidelines, not fixed quotas. The plant's concept decides its set. Higher rarity should generally mean higher ability strength, greater complexity, more impressive presentation and a more valuable discovery.

Do not make higher rarity merely a cosmetic upgrade. A Relic or Origin plant must feel meaningfully different in actual gameplay.

## Set unlock structure

A proposed set progression:
1. Discover and scan the plant.
2. Complete its first research milestone to unlock the set's signature ability.
3. Continue research to unlock the remaining abilities one by one.
4. Complete a final mastery milestone to fully understand the set and unlock its complete presentation or special interaction.

This gives each plant a longer discovery journey and makes research meaningful.

## Ability presentation: epic by default

Every ability should have:
- A distinct activation animation.
- Strong, recognisable visual effects.
- A satisfying sound identity.
- Clear impact feedback.
- A memorable visual silhouette.
- A readable cooldown and status indicator.
- A special signature effect for its plant set.

Visual intensity should rise with rarity. Natural abilities can still look beautiful and satisfying; Relic and Origin abilities should deliver major spectacle. Use different visual languages rather than making everything an explosion: roots, petals, spores, wind, water, sound waves, frost, stone, refraction, light and living energy.

Clarity matters. Players must still be able to read the environment, understand attack areas and see other players during spectacular effects. Include quality settings for effects intensity.

## Loadout model

**Proposal:** Players equip complete plant ability sets, then select a limited number of abilities from their unlocked sets for a field loadout.

Possible structure:
- Several plant sets can be equipped or prepared.
- Each set has a signature identity and its own ability slots.
- Players can switch prepared sets at designated safe locations or between expeditions.
- Cooldowns and active effects are handled consistently.
- A player should be able to build a loadout around exploration, encounters or a balanced mix.

Exact number of equipped sets, active slots, swapping rules and cooldown model remain TBD.

## Combat direction

Attacking is a core pillar, not a minor extra. Plant powers should create satisfying, varied encounter options:
- Focused attacks.
- Area-control effects.
- Traps and binding.
- Defensive and counter effects.
- Disruption and utility.
- Movement attacks and combinations.

Combat should be designed around readable fictional game mechanics, not realistic weapon simulation. Whether encounters are PvE, PvP, or both remains undecided.

## Balance principles

- Higher rarity generally means greater power, but cooldowns, duration, range, conditions and commitment provide balance.
- Avoid making rare plants mandatory for basic world progression.
- Common sets should remain useful through the whole game.
- Strong visual effects must not obscure other players or important environmental clues.
- Different sets should offer different strengths rather than one universal best choice.
- Test every ability on keyboard/mouse, controller and mobile.
- Do not sell direct competitive power as a monetisation shortcut.
