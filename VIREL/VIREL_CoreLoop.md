# VIREL — Core Gameplay Loop

## Goal
Make exploration, plant discovery, ability mastery and encounters reinforce one another.

## Primary loop

1. **Prepare:** choose plant ability sets and the abilities to bring on the expedition.
2. **Travel:** explore a large connected world and learn its landmarks, routes and habitats.
3. **Observe:** notice unusual plants, environmental clues, tracks, spores, sounds or growth patterns.
4. **Scan:** identify the organism and reveal available research leads.
5. **Investigate:** use context-appropriate interactions such as observing, sampling, comparing, photographing or testing environmental conditions.
6. **Document:** add the discovery and evidence to the botanical archive.
7. **Research:** complete the species research path to unlock its themed ability set, with abilities revealed across milestones.
8. **Master:** learn how the set's exploration, attack and botanical abilities work together.
9. **Apply:** use those abilities to reach hidden areas, overcome environmental challenges and take on encounters.
10. **Push onward:** discover new habitats, rare plants and worlds that expand the player's collection of powers.

## The three connected pillars

### Exploration
- Large worlds with distinct biomes and hidden sub-biomes.
- Environmental clues, vertical routes, caves, underwater regions and secrets.
- Abilities that open alternate routes and new ways to traverse familiar places.

### Attacking and encounters
- Plant-powered attacks with clear impact and satisfying feedback.
- Varied encounter roles such as focused attacks, area control, traps, defence, disruption and mobility.
- Encounters should give players reasons to learn and combine different plant sets.
- Exact enemy types, PvE structure and PvP rules remain TBD.

### Plant discovery and abilities
- Every plant has its own identity, habitat, rarity and research path.
- Each plant unlocks a multi-ability set rather than one isolated perk.
- Higher rarity generally means stronger, more complex and more visually spectacular sets.
- Ability research should feel like a major progression reward.

## Make exploration meaningful

- Give players multiple clues rather than a single glowing waypoint.
- Allow some species to be found through observation rather than a map marker.
- Let weather, time, region and local ecology influence discoveries.
- Provide useful discoveries at every rarity tier.
- Reward careful investigation, not only luck.
- Make revisiting a location worthwhile when the player's tools, knowledge or environmental conditions change.

## Expedition rhythm

A good session should contain a mixture of:
- Short discoveries close to a known path.
- Medium investigations requiring a few steps or a return visit.
- Long-form rare discoveries involving several clues or environmental conditions.
- Encounters that reward learning different ability sets.
- Optional landmarks, hidden areas and scenic points of interest.

## Failure and friction

Research can require thought without becoming tedious. Avoid long unskippable scanning animations, excessive repeated button prompts, arbitrary real-time waits and progress loss from disconnects.

Exact interaction verbs, research costs, ability slots, cooldowns, encounter structure and session length are TBD.
