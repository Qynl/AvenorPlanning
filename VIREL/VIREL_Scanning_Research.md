# VIREL — Scanning and Research

## Design objective
The scanner should be a central exploration tool. It turns observation into useful information and gives players a reason to investigate rather than simply collect.

## Scanner states

1. **Unregistered:** organism is not yet identified.
2. **Detected:** scanner recognises a biological signature but cannot classify it.
3. **Preliminary scan:** reveals a tentative name, broad type and possible habitat clues.
4. **Documented:** player has gathered enough evidence to add a reliable entry to the archive.
5. **Studied:** additional research reveals properties, interactions and potential ability applications.
6. **Mastered:** optional deeper research completes the species record and grants a mastery reward.

Exact stage names and thresholds are proposals.

## Scan information

A scan may reveal:
- Species identity or partial classification.
- Rarity classification, when known.
- Habitat and environmental preferences.
- Growth cycle or active time.
- Nearby ecological relationships.
- Safe and effective interaction hints.
- Research leads and unknown properties.

The scanner should not reveal every secret immediately. Information can be progressively uncovered.

## Field interactions

Potential interaction verbs:
- Observe: watch growth or behaviour over a short period.
- Sample: collect a limited research sample.
- Photograph: document a visual or behavioural state.
- Measure: record temperature, light, moisture or another relevant condition.
- Compare: analyse two related organisms or samples.
- Stimulate: interact with a plant using a suitable tool or environmental condition.

Only interactions that fit the species should be required. Avoid making every plant use the same sequence of prompts.

## Research

Research converts evidence into discoveries. Possible outputs:
- Confirmed species information.
- New scanner capabilities.
- Ability unlocks or ability upgrades.
- New habitat clues.
- Archive completion rewards.
- Cosmetic titles, badges or specimen displays.

Research should use understandable evidence requirements. Players should be able to see what they know, what is missing and why an experiment might help.

## Anti-friction principles

- Do not force players to repeat a full scan on every encounter.
- Preserve documented progress across sessions.
- Make sample limits and consumption explicit.
- Keep scanner UI readable on mobile and desktop.
- Avoid making rare discovery depend on a single obscure button interaction.
- Provide accessibility options for scan effects, audio cues and colour-dependent information.

## Open decisions

Scanner form factor, upgrade slots, battery/energy, sample inventory, research station location, co-op research credit and exact UI flow are TBD.
