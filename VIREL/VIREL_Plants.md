# VIREL — Plant Catalogue and Rarity

## Purpose
Plants are the primary collectible and discovery content of VIREL. Each species should be designed as a complete gameplay object, not just a mesh with a rarity label.

## Species data template

Every designed species should eventually define:
- Common name and scientific-style name.
- Rarity.
- Visual silhouette, palette, animation and audio identity.
- Habitat and preferred environmental conditions.
- Discovery clues and spawn/discovery rules.
- Scanner results and research stages.
- Field interaction(s) and sample rules.
- Research outcomes.
- Ability or non-ability reward.
- Encyclopedia text, facts and completion criteria.
- Performance and replication requirements.

## Proposed rarity ladder

The following is a proposal, not yet locked:

1. **Common** — frequent foundational species; still useful for learning and research.
2. **Uncommon** — distinctive habitat, appearance or interaction.
3. **Rare** — requires more deliberate searching or a particular condition.
4. **Epic** — unusual design and a more involved discovery.
5. **Legendary** — memorable, difficult-to-find species with substantial research.
6. **Mythical** — exceptional discoveries tied to unusual conditions or secrets.
7. **Celestial** — extremely rare, world-defining botanical phenomena.
8. **Unknown** — unclassified organisms whose rules are not fully understood.

Rarity should communicate discovery difficulty and uniqueness, not raw combat or movement power. A common species can still unlock a useful ability, while a rare species can provide knowledge, a cosmetic archive reward or a special interaction.

## Discovery model

Avoid pure random rarity rolls as the only source of excitement. Mix:
- Habitat-based distribution.
- Environmental conditions such as rain, temperature and time.
- Seasonal or rotating world states.
- Multi-step clues and hidden micro-habitats.
- Rare variants and unusual growth patterns.
- Multi-species relationships, such as one species growing near another.
- A small amount of controlled randomness to keep expeditions fresh.

All rare-spawn systems should be fair, observable enough to learn, and not dependent on paid boosts.

## Variants

Variants may change colour, size, pattern, behaviour, research properties or archive value. Keep a clear distinction between:
- A separate species.
- A visual variant of an existing species.
- A temporary environmental state.
- A rare mutation with its own discovery record.

## Collection rules to decide

- Can a player keep physical specimens?
- Are samples consumed by research?
- Can a species be rediscovered for repeatable materials?
- Are there specimen quality grades?
- Can players trade, display or grow specimens?
- How are limited-time species recorded after an event?
