# VIREL — World and Biomes

## World vision

VIREL is built around **multiple enormous worlds that rotate and change over time**. The available world roster is not permanently fixed: new worlds can arrive after a number of days, and existing worlds may rotate out.

The current first-universe proposal contains **10 distinct worlds**, each with multiple major biomes and hidden sub-biomes. The full catalogue, signatures and sub-region concepts are in [VIREL_World_Catalogue.md](VIREL_World_Catalogue.md).

## Confirmed world structure

- Multiple enormous worlds, rather than one single permanent map.
- Worlds can change over time.
- A new world can arrive after several days.
- Each world contains its own biomes and ecosystems.
- Worlds can contain hidden sub-regions, including underground ecosystems, deep caves, secret islands and underwater areas.
- Exact rotation timing, number of active worlds and return/archive rules are TBD.

**Design safeguard:** World rotation should create anticipation, not punish players for missing a short window. Important progression and core discoveries need a fair way to be revisited, archived or recovered.

## Visual direction

**Stylized realism** is confirmed.

- Believable natural environments and readable terrain.
- Expressive, slightly exaggerated botanical designs.
- Vibrant but controlled colors.
- Strong natural lighting, atmosphere, shadows and environmental effects.
- Rare discoveries can have more extraordinary visual presentation, while ordinary environments must remain beautiful and visually interesting.

Visual effects should preserve contrast and rarity. If every plant glows dramatically, special discoveries stop feeling special.

## Shared biome design rules

- Every biome needs a distinct visual identity and ecological behaviour.
- Sub-biomes must introduce a meaningful change in habitat, traversal, atmosphere or discovery rules.
- Use landmarks to help players form a mental map.
- Hidden areas should reward observation and investigation, not random wall-hugging.
- Environmental conditions should be readable enough that players can learn why a species appears.
- Design vertical, underground, underwater and surface routes as appropriate to each world.

## Environmental systems

Potential world variables:

- Time of day.
- Weather and storms.
- Temperature and humidity.
- Light exposure and shade.
- Water level and local moisture.
- Wind and altitude.
- Seasonal or event states.
- Nearby species and ecological relationships.

Use a controlled set of meaningful variables rather than simulating every environmental detail. The player should be able to understand why a plant appears or behaves differently.

## World discovery

- Landmarks should help players build a mental map.
- Hidden micro-locations should reward leaving main paths.
- Natural routes, caves, canopy paths, underwater passages and vertical spaces should create variety.
- Some discoveries should require returning with new abilities or knowledge.
- The map should support solo exploration and optional shared expeditions.

## World scale and streaming

Each world should feel enormous, but exact dimensions are TBD. Establish performance budgets before building every biome. Use streaming, modular terrain and region-based content loading where appropriate.

## Live world states

Weather, environmental events and special world states can temporarily alter habitats and reveal new discoveries. These systems should complement the larger world rotation, not make core species permanently missable.

## Rotation safeguards

- Permanent discoveries and research progress must persist when a world rotates out.
- Essential progression must not depend on a single short availability window.
- Returning worlds can gain new sub-biomes, variants or changed environmental states.
- Exact active-world count, rotation schedule and archive access are still TBD.
